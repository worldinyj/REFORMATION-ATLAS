## Exact distinctions

IPO **Article V §3** specifically describes the confessional balance in four imperial cities: Augsburg, Dinkelsbühl, Biberach and Ravensburg. This is not the same as **Article V §35**, which regulates merchant/craft/guild membership, inheritance, hospitals, charity, cemeteries and burials for historically defined subjects. The legal English excerpt is from German History in Documents and Images.

Augsburg hospital/foundation and office parity is an **institutional context** and does NOT prove a named V §35 admission or inheritance lawsuit. Biberach's shared St Martin church began in **1548**, while its municipal parity was implemented in **1649**. Dinkelsbühl's 1653 Latin school and 1654 school festival dates are from a local festival society retrospective and await school/council manuscript crosscheck. Ravensburg's 1648 confessional legal equality is supported by the official city chronicle, not an adjudicated guild case. Münster's Protestant burial disputes date to **1587 and the 1590s**, and are **antecedents**, never outcomes of 1648 treaty implementation.
